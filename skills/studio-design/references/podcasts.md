# Podcast preset

Use this preset for a conversational episode or a requested solo podcast. Follow the [shared source-to-audio workflow](source-to-audio.md) for evidence, duration, voice production, reuse, measurement and delivery. This recipe guides the script; it does not select an engine, language or voice.

## Editorial direction

- For conversation, give presenters complementary jobs: explain a mechanism, ask a useful listener question, test a distinction or summarize an implication. Either presenter may explain or question when the argument needs it.
- A useful progression is a source-supported tension, a necessary listener question, a concrete mechanism or example, and a limitation that changes the listener's understanding. Adapt it to the material rather than applying a fixed sequence to every topic.
- Vary turn length. Let one idea lead into the next; avoid a rigid question/answer rhythm, repetitive agreement, artificial surprise or announcing every chapter.
- Introduce concepts before using conversational shorthand. Use examples and analogies that preserve the source's scope; identify hypothetical illustrations as such.
- Close with a grounded synthesis. A reflective editorial question can be useful when requested or appropriate, but do not invent disagreement or factual claims for drama.

For solo episodes, use direct sentences, signposts and short recaps after dense explanations. Define unfamiliar terms before using them. Do not add a second presenter solely because the preset is called podcast.

## Rendering and review

Keep ordered turns in the shared script, with stable speaker roles and source references. Map roles to user-selected voices in the production handoff. A native dialogue capability may keep coherent exchanges together; single-speaker capabilities may schedule blocks by voice and reassemble their original order. Real alignment is needed to split a grouped render.

Use a short complete explanation for pilots. Check whether the speakers respond to each other, the episode earns its duration, transitions sound natural and sources retain their qualifications. A reference episode can guide structure; its undiarized transcript does not establish speaker identity or voice delivery. Follow the shared workflow's local helper contract and voice/format fallbacks.
